\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

HeatGeo treats the teacher embedding space as a sampled semantic manifold and transfers its geometry through heat diffusion, instantiating the analysis of Section~\ref{sec:theory}. Section~\ref{sec:graph} builds the discrete manifold, Section~\ref{sec:kernels} defines a family of diffusion kernels indexed by diffusion time, Section~\ref{sec:objective} trains the student to reproduce all of them at once, and Section~\ref{sec:walk} extends supervision from anchor rows to the rows a random walk visits.

\subsection{Teacher Semantic Graph}
\label{sec:graph}

Let $\mathcal{D}=\{x_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$ be the unlabeled distillation corpus, and let $t_i=T(x_i)$ denote the cached embedding produced by teacher encoder $T$. For each example, we retrieve its top-$k$ neighbors under teacher cosine similarity:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{N}_k^T(i)
    =
    \operatorname{TopK}_{j\neq i}\cos(t_i,t_j).
\end{equation}
To reduce noisy one-directional edges and hubness, HeatGeo retains only mutual $k$NN connections:
\begin{equation}
    j \in \mathcal{M}_k^T(i)
    \Longleftrightarrow
    j \in \mathcal{N}_k^T(i)
    \ \mathrm{and}\
    i \in \mathcal{N}_k^T(j).
\end{equation}
The weighted adjacency matrix $W^T$ is
\begin{equation}
    W_{ij}^{T}
    =
    \begin{cases}
    \exp\!\left(\cos(t_i,t_j)/\tau_g\right),
        & j \in \mathcal{M}_k^T(i),\\
    0,  & \mathrm{otherwise},
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $\tau_g$ controls graph sharpness. With diagonal degree matrix $D^T$, the row-normalized transition matrix is
\begin{equation}
    P^T=(D^T)^{-1}W^T,
\end{equation}
and each row $P_i^T$ defines a one-step semantic random-walk distribution from anchor $x_i$.

\subsection{A Multi-Scale Family of Teacher Kernels}
\label{sec:kernels}

Diffusion time is the natural scale parameter of a manifold. On a continuous manifold the heat kernel $h_t(u,v)$ interpolates between the ambient metric as $t\to 0$, where $h_t(u,v)\propto\exp(-d(u,v)^2/4t)$ depends only on the distance between the two points, and global connectivity as $t$ grows, where mass has travelled along the manifold and reflects its intrinsic structure. HeatGeo distills this entire interpolation rather than a single point on it, by pairing the graph walks with the ambient kernel they emerge from.

\paragraph{Graph scales ($r\geq 1$).}
Powers of the lazy walk $\tilde{P}^T=\tfrac12\big(I+P^T\big)$ expose progressively broader semantic regions,
\begin{equation}
    P_r^T=\big(\tilde{P}^T\big)^r,
    \qquad r\in\{1,2,4\},
\end{equation}
where the lazy step retains half of the mass in place---the discrete analogue of a heat-semigroup step---and small $r$ preserves sharp local neighborhoods while larger $r$ captures multi-hop connectivity and community structure. Diffusion is computed sparsely by propagating probability mass through graph neighbor lists and retaining the highest-probability columns at each step (Lemma~\ref{lem:truncation}).

\paragraph{Ambient scale ($r=0$).}
Before any propagation, the teacher metric itself induces a distribution. Writing this small-time limit of the heat kernel in terms of cosine similarity gives
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:ambient}
    p_{i,0}^{T}(j)
    =
    \frac{\exp\!\left(\cos(t_i,t_j)/\tau_0\right)}
    {\sum_{j'}\exp\!\left(\cos(t_i,t_{j'})/\tau_0\right)}.
\end{equation}
This scale is defined for every pair in the corpus, whereas the graph scales are supported on the mutual-$k$NN structure. It is what makes the family an interpolation with two endpoints instead of a set of walks: the graph scales describe how mass \emph{moves} on the manifold, the ambient scale describes the metric it moves on. Because teacher embeddings are already cached for graph construction, Eq.~\ref{eq:ambient} is obtained at no additional cost --- no teacher forward pass, no stored hidden states, and no trainable parameters.

We write $\mathcal{R}=\{0,1,2,4\}$ for the full scale family.

\subsection{Candidate Sets and Batch-Level Sharing}
\label{sec:candidates}

Distributions over the full corpus are intractable, so each anchor is scored against a candidate set whose induced error Proposition~\ref{prop:sampling} bounds,
\begin{equation}
    C_i=C_i^{+}\cup C_i^{h}\cup C_i^{r},
\end{equation}
combining high-probability diffusion neighbors $C_i^{+}$, teacher hard negatives $C_i^{h}$ mined from same-source nearest neighbors outside the graph, and random negatives $C_i^{r}$. Candidate sets are redrawn every epoch from precomputed pools, so the student is evaluated against fresh comparisons rather than repeatedly refitting one frozen sample. The graph target at scale $r\geq1$ is the diffusion mass restricted and renormalized over this set,
\begin{equation}
    p_{i,r}^{T}(j)
    =
    \frac{(P_r^T)_{ij}}
    {\sum_{j'\in C_i}(P_r^T)_{ij'}},
    \qquad j\in C_i,
\end{equation}
which expresses graded semantic relatedness rather than a binary positive--negative label.

Within a mini-batch $B$, the candidate sets are pooled: every anchor is scored against
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{P}_B=\bigcup_{i\in B}C_i,
\end{equation}
deduplicated by corpus index and with the anchor's own column removed. Since $\mathcal{P}_B$ is encoded once for the whole batch, this multiplies the comparisons available per anchor by roughly $|B|$ at unchanged encoding cost, and the shared columns couple anchors so that similarity levels become comparable across the batch --- the property that semantic textual similarity and a single global cosine threshold both depend on. The ambient scale is normalized over $\mathcal{P}_B$, where Eq.~\ref{eq:ambient} assigns teacher mass to every column, and the graph scales are normalized over $C_i$, the support on which the walk from anchor $i$ is defined.

\subsection{Diffusion Objective}
\label{sec:objective}

Let $s_i=S_{\theta}(x_i)$ be the student embedding of anchor $x_i$. At each scale the student induces a distribution over the corresponding column set $\Omega_{i,r}$ ($\mathcal{P}_B$ for $r=0$, $C_i$ otherwise),
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:student}
    p_{i,r}^{S}(j)
    =
    \frac{\exp\!\left(\cos(s_i,s_j)/\tau_r\right)}
    {\sum_{j'\in\Omega_{i,r}}\exp\!\left(\cos(s_i,s_{j'})/\tau_r\right)},
    \qquad j\in\Omega_{i,r},
\end{equation}
and HeatGeo minimizes the weighted divergence to the teacher family,
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:ldiff}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{diff}}
    =
    \frac{1}{|B|}
    \sum_{i\in B}
    \sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}
    \omega_r
    D_{\mathrm{KL}}
    \left(
        p_{i,r}^{T}\,\|\,p_{i,r}^{S}
    \right),
\end{equation}
with $\omega_r$ normalized scale weights that emphasize the ambient and local scales.

\paragraph{Resolution-dependent student distributions.}
Eq.~\ref{eq:student} gives each scale its own temperature $\tau_r$, increasing with $r$ so that broader teacher targets are matched at a correspondingly smoother resolution. This is what makes the family informative rather than redundant: by Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse}, a shared student distribution reduces the multi-scale objective to a single-scale one against the mixture $\bar{p}^T_i$, so scales that differ only in the teacher would leave the gradient unchanged. Distinct $\tau_r$ break this equivalence. Matching the same similarities at several resolutions is over-determined, so the student must reproduce the teacher's cosine \emph{gaps} rather than only its ranking, which is precisely the information that graded similarity tasks measure.

\paragraph{A one-parameter temperature law.}
Not every member of the ladder is a design choice, and the rest follow one law. The sharpest graph scale's target is exactly the transition row --- the lazy walk at $r=1$ is $\tfrac12(I+P^T)$, whose off-diagonal part renormalizes to $P^T_i$, itself a softmax of teacher cosines at $\tau_g$ --- so by Proposition~\ref{prop:temptie} it must be matched at the graph temperature, $\tau_1=\tau_g$: at any other value the term's zeros are an affine rescaling of the teacher's cosines, incompatible with the ambient scale, and the objective acquires an artificial floor. For the broader scales the heat kernel fixes the functional form: on the unit sphere $d^2=2-2\cos$, so the small-time kernel $\exp(-d^2/4t)$ of Section~\ref{sec:kernels} is a softmax in cosine similarity at temperature $2t$, and diffusion time grows linearly in $r$, giving $\tau_r\propto r$. We therefore generate the whole ladder from a single exponent,
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:templaw}
    \tau_r=\tau_g\,r^{\alpha},
    \qquad
    \tau_0=\tau_g\,r_{\max}^{\alpha},
\end{equation}
where $\alpha=1$ is the value the heat-kernel argument prescribes --- exact when the student embeds the manifold isometrically and the kernel is in its Gaussian regime --- and $r=1$ recovers the tie for every $\alpha$. The ambient scale sits at the top of the ladder, since it calibrates over the pooled column set, the broadest comparison in the objective, and it uses $\tau_0$ on both the teacher (Eq.~\ref{eq:ambient}) and the student (Eq.~\ref{eq:student}) side, the same-temperature convention of knowledge distillation \citep{hinton2015distilling}; the walk term is tied to $\tau_g$ in Section~\ref{sec:walk}. We adopt the heat-kernel value $\alpha=1$ throughout, so temperatures contribute no tuned parameter at all: the full ladder follows from $\tau_g$ and the scale set.

\subsection{Walk-Sampled Kernel Matching}
\label{sec:walk}

Every row of the teacher operator that Eq.~\ref{eq:ldiff} matches is indexed by an \emph{anchor}. The rows at the remaining corpus nodes are never supervised, even though a large number of them are already encoded: the pooled set $\mathcal{P}_B$ contains, on every step, several hundred non-anchor texts whose student embeddings are computed and then used only as columns. Walk-sampled kernel matching supervises those rows as well, at no additional encoding cost.

\paragraph{Which rows.}
From each anchor $i\in B$ we run $M$ random walks of length $L$ on the teacher transition matrix,
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:walk}
    j^{(m)}_{0}=i,
    \qquad
    j^{(m)}_{t+1}\sim P^{T}_{j^{(m)}_{t}},
    \qquad
    t=0,\dots,L-1,
\end{equation}
and take the nodes visited at steps $t\geq1$ as the rows to supervise. Writing $\nu_B$ for the normalized visit counts over the batch --- the walk's occupancy measure, restricted to nodes present in $\mathcal{P}_B$ --- the walk selects rows in proportion to how much diffusion mass reaches them from the batch's anchors, which is the same principle that selects columns in Section~\ref{sec:candidates}. Visited nodes not already in $C_i$ replace random negatives, so the pooled set covers the walk without enlarging it.

\paragraph{Which columns.}
For a visited node $j$ the column set is the teacher's own support for that row, intersected with what the batch holds,
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:walkcols}
    \Omega^{w}_{j}=\mathcal{P}_B\cap\mathcal{M}_k^{T}(j),
\end{equation}
and the teacher target is the transition row restricted to it, $P^{T}_{j}\big|_{\Omega^{w}_{j}}$. Restricting to $\mathcal{M}_k^{T}(j)$ rather than to all of $\mathcal{P}_B$ is what keeps the term compatible with Section~\ref{sec:candidates}: on $\Omega^w_j$ every column carries teacher mass, so no column receives a target of zero for the accidental reason that it was drawn for a different anchor. A hard negative, in particular, is by construction a high-similarity pair excluded from the mutual-$k$NN graph, so it can never enter $\Omega^w_j$ and is never pushed apart by this term.

\paragraph{The objective.}
With student distributions $p^{S}_{w,j}(j')\propto\exp\big(\cos(s_j,s_{j'})/\tau_w\big)$ on $\Omega^{w}_{j}$,
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:lwalk}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{walk}}
    =
    \sum_{j}
    \nu_B(j)\,
    D_{\mathrm{KL}}
    \left(
        P^{T}_{j}\big|_{\Omega^{w}_{j}}
        \,\Big\|\,
        p^{S}_{w,j}
    \right),
    \qquad
    \tau_w=\tau_g,
\end{equation}
where tying the student temperature to the graph temperature makes the teacher target exactly attainable: $P^T_j$ is itself a softmax of teacher cosines at $\tau_g$, so Eq.~\ref{eq:lwalk} has an infimum of $0$ reached precisely when student and teacher cosines agree on $\Omega^w_j$ up to a constant --- the walk-row instance of Proposition~\ref{prop:temptie}, and a tie the implementation enforces rather than exposes. The term is therefore on the same scale as Eq.~\ref{eq:ldiff}, and $\lambda$ below trades off two divergences rather than converting units.

\paragraph{Dense targets rather than sampled successors.}
A natural alternative scores the sampled transitions themselves, $-\log p^{S}(j_{t+1}\mid j_{t})$ summed along each path. Because the walk is first-order and each step is scored on the teacher's own $j_t$, that path likelihood factorizes into independent one-step terms, and its expectation over the walk is exactly Eq.~\ref{eq:lwalk}. The sampled form is thus an unbiased but high-variance estimator of the dense one; since $P^{T}_{j}$ is already materialized during graph construction, we use the dense form and pay no variance. It also fixes what the term does and does not claim: it matches one-step kernels at walk-visited rows, and nothing in it couples two consecutive steps.

\paragraph{Why step $0$ is dropped.}
The walk starts at the anchor, but the anchor's row is already supervised: the lazy walk at $r=1$ is $\tfrac12(I+P^T)$, whose off-diagonal part renormalizes to exactly $P^{T}_{i}$. Supervising step $0$ would distil the $r=1$ target a second time, so only $t\geq1$ contributes.

\paragraph{Full objective.}
\begin{equation}
    \boxed{
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{HeatGeo}}
    =
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{diff}}
    +
    \lambda\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{walk}}
    }
\end{equation}
Beyond its role as extra supervision, the walk term removes most of the gauge freedom that Proposition~\ref{prop:gauge} identifies. Each traversed edge constrains two rows that contain each other as columns, and cosine symmetry then forces their similarity offsets to agree; chaining along the walk propagates the identification through the graph, leaving one offset per connected component instead of one per anchor (Proposition~\ref{prop:walkgauge}). This is the sense in which the trajectory, and not merely the set of visited nodes, does work: it is the path that links the constraints.

Training therefore requires only cached teacher embeddings. HeatGeo uses no teacher hidden states, no online teacher forward pass, no task labels, no contrastive or layer-alignment objective, and no change to the student architecture.
