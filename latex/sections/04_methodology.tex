\section{RIPPLE: Corpus-Anchored Geometry Distillation}
\label{sec:method}

RIPPLE replaces relations selected by mini-batch co-occurrence with rows selected from a teacher-induced corpus operator. It then transfers those rows at several geometric scales, approximates them on small candidate sets, and supervises overlapping rows to improve cross-anchor consistency. For distributions $p,q$ on a common finite set, let $\mathrm{TV}(p,q)=\tfrac12\lVert p-q\rVert_1$; for a column set $C$, let $p|_C$ denote restriction to $C$ followed by renormalization. Proofs are in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\subsection{Teacher Neighborhood Operator}
\label{sec:graph}

Let $t_i=T(x_i)$ be the cached, unit-normalized embedding of corpus example $x_i$. Writing $\mathcal{N}_k^T(i)$ for its top-$k$ neighbors under teacher cosine similarity, RIPPLE retains mutual edges,
\begin{equation}
    j\in\mathcal{M}_k^T(i)
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    j\in\mathcal{N}_k^T(i)
    \ \text{and}\
    i\in\mathcal{N}_k^T(j),
\end{equation}
which suppresses one-directional hub edges. The weighted adjacency and transition operator are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:teacher-operator}
    W^T_{ij}
    =
    \mathbf{1}\!\left[j\in\mathcal{M}_k^T(i)\right]
    \exp\!\left(c^T_{ij}/\tau_i\right),
    \qquad
    P^T=(D^T)^{-1}W^T,
\end{equation}
where $D^T$ is the diagonal degree matrix. The main experiments use a fixed bandwidth $\tau_i=\tau_g$ for every row. The implementation also supports entropic per-row bandwidths with fixed target perplexity~\citep{hinton2002sne,maaten2008tsne,vladymyrov2013entropic}; all results below apply row-wise and require only that the student use the bandwidth with which the corresponding teacher row was constructed. Isolated nodes fall back to their directed teacher neighbors. After this preprocessing step, the teacher model is no longer needed.

Each row $P_i^T$ is a teacher-selected conditional neighborhood distribution. Unlike an in-batch target, its support is tied to corpus geometry and is available whenever $x_i$ is sampled as an anchor. We use the lazy operator $\widetilde P^T=\tfrac12(I+P^T)$ to define longer walks without periodicity.

\subsection{Multi-Scale Diffusion Distillation}
\label{sec:kernels}

The sharpest graph target is the one-step row $p^T_{i,1}=P_i^T$. Broader targets are obtained from the lazy teacher walk,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:diffusion-targets}
    p^T_{i,r}
    =
    \left.
    \delta_i^\top(\widetilde P^T)^r
    \right|_{\mathcal D\setminus\{i\}},
    \qquad r\in\{2,4\},
\end{equation}
where the trivial self column is removed and the remaining mass is renormalized. This construction has the interpretation of diffusion on a sampled manifold~\citep{coifman2006diffusionmaps}, but the method and results concern the finite teacher operator directly.

The student does not construct a second graph during training. Its one-step row is read from its cosine scores on the \emph{teacher} support and bandwidth,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:student-operator}
    P_i^S
    =
    \operatorname{softmax}_{j\in\mathcal M_k^T(i)}
    \!\left(c^S_{ij}/\tau_i\right).
\end{equation}
Matching this local object controls the diffusion generated by the student rows on the same support.

\begin{theorem}[Local-to-global operator transfer]
\label{thm:global}
Let $P^T$ and $P^S$ be teacher and student transition matrices on the same support. Suppose that, for every row $i$, one-step matching on a candidate set $C_i$ satisfies
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_i^T|_{C_i}\,\|\,P_i^S|_{C_i})\leq\varepsilon$,
while $P_i^T(C_i)\geq1-\delta_T$ and $P_i^S(C_i)\geq1-\delta_S$. Then, for every $r\geq1$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:local-global-bound}
    \max_i
    \mathrm{TV}\!\left(
        \delta_i^\top(\widetilde P^T)^r,
        \delta_i^\top(\widetilde P^S)^r
    \right)
    \leq
    \frac r2
    \left(\sqrt{\varepsilon/2}+\delta_T+\delta_S\right).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

Thus one-step supervision is sufficient for conditional local-to-global control, but its worst-case bound grows linearly with diffusion time. RIPPLE additionally asks the same student cosine field to predict the teacher's broader rows. At scale $r$, define the full student score readout
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:scale-readout}
    q^S_{i,r}(j)
    \propto
    \exp\!\left(c^S_{ij}/\tau_r(i)\right),
    \qquad j\neq i.
\end{equation}
Unlike $(\widetilde P^S)^r$, this is a scale-specific readout of student scores rather than a separately materialized student diffusion. It provides direct supervision at the teacher's broader neighborhood scale.

\begin{corollary}[Direct multi-scale tightening]
\label{cor:multiscale}
If at a scale $r$ the restricted divergence satisfies
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p^T_{i,r}|_{C_i}\,\|\,q^S_{i,r}|_{C_i})\leq\varepsilon_r$
and the teacher and student score rows place at most $\delta_T$ and $\delta_S$ mass outside $C_i$, then
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{TV}(p^T_{i,r},q^S_{i,r})
    \leq
    \sqrt{\varepsilon_r/2}+\delta_T+\delta_S,
\end{equation}
with no propagation factor $r$.
\end{corollary}

The corollary does not claim that a softmax readout equals the student's $r$-step Markov row. It states the narrower fact used by the objective: direct scale-$r$ supervision avoids relying only on the propagated one-step bound. Section~\ref{sec:analysis} tests whether this surrogate also reduces the measured student-operator diffusion error at held-out scales.

Different teacher diffusion times cannot be matched meaningfully by one shared student distribution. For the graph scales we use a common candidate domain $C_i$ and scale-specific resolutions $\tau_r(i)=\sqrt r\,\tau_i$, with $\tau_1(i)=\tau_i$.

\begin{proposition}[Collapse under a shared resolution]
\label{prop:collapse}
Let all graph scales for anchor $i$ share the same student distribution $q_i^S$ on the same support, and let $\sum_r\omega_r=1$. Then
\begin{equation}
    \sum_r\omega_rD_{\mathrm{KL}}(p^T_{i,r}\,\|\,q_i^S)
    =
    H\!\left(\sum_r\omega_rp^T_{i,r},q_i^S\right)
    -
    \sum_r\omega_rH(p^T_{i,r}),
\end{equation}
so optimization is equivalent to matching the single mixture $\sum_r\omega_rp^T_{i,r}$.
\end{proposition}

Scale-specific temperatures make the student distributions distinct functions of the same cosine scores, preventing this algebraic collapse. The temperature tie at $r=1$ and the remaining ladder are discussed in Appendix~\ref{app:hyper}.

\subsection{Efficient Candidate Sampling}
\label{sec:candidates}

The support of an $r$-step teacher row can be much larger than $k$, so scoring every anchor against every reachable corpus example is unnecessary and expensive. RIPPLE samples a small set from the target mass.

\begin{proposition}[Mass missed by sampled candidates]
\label{prop:sampling}
Let $p$ be a target distribution and let $C$ contain $m$ independent draws from $p$. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sampling-bound}
    \mathbb E\!\left[\mathrm{TV}(p|_C,p)\right]
    =
    \mathbb E[p(\bar C)]
    \leq
    \min_{t>0}
    \left(e^{-tm}+\mathrm{Tail}_p(t)\right),
    \qquad
    \mathrm{Tail}_p(t)=\sum_{j:p_j<t}p_j.
\end{equation}
If every supported atom has mass at least $c/\rho$, where $\rho=e^{H(p)}$ is the target perplexity, the expected missed mass is at most $e^{-cm/\rho}$.
\end{proposition}

The relevant budget is therefore the target's effective support, not the corpus size and not the raw number of nodes reachable by diffusion. In the regime studied here, this permits $m\ll k$. The implementation draws high-mass diffusion candidates without replacement using Gumbel top-$k$ sampling~\citep{efraimidis2006weighted,kool2019stochastic}, then adds teacher hard negatives and random negatives. These negatives expose the student to columns outside the retained diffusion mass and empirically control the student leakage term $\delta_S$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:global}; the theorem keeps this quantity as an explicit hypothesis.

For a mini-batch $B$, candidate sets are pooled into
$\mathcal P_B=\bigcup_{i\in B}C_i$ and deduplicated. Every pooled text is encoded once, while each anchor can be compared with the shared pool. Graph-scale targets are normalized on the anchor's own $C_i$. A dense ambient target supplies teacher similarities for the wider shared pool,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ambient}
    p^T_{i,0}(j)
    =
    \frac{\exp(c^T_{ij}/\tau_0)}
    {\sum_{j'\in\mathcal P_B\setminus\{i\}}\exp(c^T_{ij'}/\tau_0)},
    \qquad j\in\mathcal P_B\setminus\{i\}.
\end{equation}
This ambient member prevents graph-external columns from being treated as false zero-mass relations; it is included in the complete geometry loss below but is not itself a diffusion time.

\subsection{Row Supervision for Cross-Anchor Consistency}
\label{sec:walk}

Matching a normalized row determines relative scores within that row but not its additive origin. This is an ambiguity of the score-level observation map, not a claim that arbitrary shifted rows form a realizable cosine Gram matrix.

\begin{proposition}[Row-wise additive ambiguity]
\label{prop:gauge}
On a fixed column set $\Omega_i$, any row-wise softmax term is invariant under
$c^S_{ij}\mapsto c^S_{ij}+a_i$. If teacher and student softmaxes use the same temperature, $|\Omega_i|\geq2$, and the term is zero, then
\begin{equation}
    c^S_{ij}=c^T_{ij}+a_i,
    \qquad j\in\Omega_i,
\end{equation}
for some row-dependent constant $a_i$.
\end{proposition}

RIPPLE uses teacher random walks to choose additional rows whose supports overlap along teacher edges. From each anchor $i\in B$, it samples $M$ non-backtracking walks of length $L$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:walk}
    j^{(m)}_0=i,
    \qquad
    j^{(m)}_{t+1}\sim P^T_{j^{(m)}_t},
    \qquad t=0,\ldots,L-1.
\end{equation}
The anchor row at $t=0$ is already supervised by the one-step target. For every visited row $u$ at $t\geq1$, let
$\Omega_u^w=\mathcal P_B\cap\mathcal M_k^T(u)$ and let $\nu_B(u)$ be its normalized visit count. RIPPLE matches the stored teacher row on this overlap:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lrow}
    \mathcal L_{\mathrm{row}}
    =
    \sum_u\nu_B(u)
    D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(
        P_u^T|_{\Omega_u^w}
        \,\middle\|\,
        \operatorname{softmax}_{v\in\Omega_u^w}
        (c^S_{uv}/\tau_u)
    \right).
\end{equation}
Visited nodes replace random candidates when possible, so the term largely reuses texts already encoded for the batch.

\begin{proposition}[Consistency induced by overlapping rows]
\label{prop:walkgauge}
Let $\mathcal S$ be the supervised rows and construct a constraint graph $\mathcal G$ on $\mathcal S$, with an edge $\{u,v\}$ whenever $v\in\Omega_u$, $u\in\Omega_v$, and both corresponding same-temperature row terms vanish. Then the score-level offsets of Proposition~\ref{prop:gauge} satisfy $a_u=a_v$ on every edge. Hence admissible row offsets are constant on each connected component of $\mathcal G$, leaving at most one offset per component rather than one per isolated row.
\end{proposition}

The mechanism is cosine symmetry: $c^S_{uv}=c^S_{vu}$ and $c^T_{uv}=c^T_{vu}$ force the two row offsets to agree. Consecutive nodes of a walk are mutual graph neighbors and their supervised rows therefore add precisely these reciprocal constraints. At nonzero loss the statement is approximate; Appendix~\ref{app:proof-gauge} bounds edge-wise offset disagreement and records the coverage argument for walk-selected rows.

\subsection{Full Objective and Complexity}
\label{sec:objective}

Let $\mathcal R=\{0,1,2,4\}$, with $\Omega_{i,0}=\mathcal P_B\setminus\{i\}$ and $\Omega_{i,r}=C_i$ for $r\geq1$. The student distribution at each scale is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:student}
    p^S_{i,r}(j)
    =
    \frac{\exp(c^S_{ij}/\tau_r(i))}
    {\sum_{j'\in\Omega_{i,r}}\exp(c^S_{ij'}/\tau_r(i))},
    \qquad j\in\Omega_{i,r},
\end{equation}
where $\tau_0$ is shared by the teacher and student ambient targets, $\tau_1(i)=\tau_i$, and $\tau_r(i)=\sqrt r\,\tau_i$ for $r>1$. With graph targets restricted to $C_i$, the anchored geometry loss is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ldiff}
    \mathcal L_{\mathrm{diff}}
    =
    \frac1{|B|}
    \sum_{i\in B}
    \sum_{r\in\mathcal R}
    \omega_r
    D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(
        p^T_{i,r}|_{\Omega_{i,r}}
        \,\|\,
        p^S_{i,r}
    \right).
\end{equation}
The ambient term $r=0$ is thus explicit inside $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{diff}}$. The complete objective is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
    \mathcal L_{\mathrm{RIPPLE}}
    =
    \mathcal L_{\mathrm{diff}}
    +
    \lambda\mathcal L_{\mathrm{row}}.
}
\end{equation}

Teacher encoding is a one-time cost. The current exact nearest-neighbor implementation computes all corpus cosine similarities in $O(N^2d_T)$ time using chunked memory, retains $O(Nk)$ graph edges, and stores sparse diffusion rows truncated by a fixed mass tolerance. Approximate nearest-neighbor search could replace the quadratic graph-build step, but is not used in the reported experiments. During student training, a batch encodes at most $|B|+|\mathcal P_B|\leq |B|(m+1)$ texts and forms $O(|B||\mathcal P_B|d_S)$ anchor--candidate similarity work. The row term reuses the pooled encodings and adds only similarities on visited-row overlaps. No teacher forward pass, teacher hidden state, task label, or additional inference-time component is required; after preprocessing, the teacher is discarded.
