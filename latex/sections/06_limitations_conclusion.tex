\section{Limitations and Conclusion}

RIPPLE turns a corpus-level geometry target into tractable supervision for a standard text encoder. The coverage analysis explains why batch-local relations under-identify teacher neighborhoods, while the diffusion and sampling results connect the implemented objective back to corpus geometry. Empirically, the strongest and most consistent gains occur on semantic textual similarity; less uniform pair-classification results indicate that geometry preservation does not benefit every task equally.

The method can inherit errors and domain bias from the teacher graph, and its preprocessing is corpus-dependent. Exact neighbor search is quadratic before sparsification, although approximate search can replace it at larger scale. Theorem~\ref{thm:profile-geometry} controls diffusion geometry rather than every property of an embedding space, while Corollary~\ref{cor:sampled-topology} retains an explicit student-leakage term. RIPPLE nevertheless adds no inference-time machinery: deployment uses only the distilled text encoder.
