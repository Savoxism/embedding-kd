\section{Objective Constraints and Implementation Notes}
\label{app:hyper}

This appendix collects material deferred from Section~\ref{sec:method}: the constraint that fixes the student temperature at any term whose target is a transition row, and three details of the graph and walk construction.

\subsection{Temperatures tied to transition-row targets}
\label{app:hyper-temptie}

Section~\ref{sec:objective} gives each scale its own student temperature. Two terms have none to give: the sharpest graph scale and the row term both match transition rows, and Theorem~\ref{thm:global} and Proposition~\ref{prop:walkgauge} are statements about the student's row at the graph's own bandwidth. Matching at any other temperature would leave those results describing a different object than the one optimized. The proposition below records what goes wrong directly at the level of the loss.

\begin{proposition}[Temperature ties]
\label{prop:temptie}
Let $\Omega$ be a column set with $|\Omega|\geq2$, and consider a term with target $\mathrm{softmax}_{\Omega}(c^T_{i\cdot}/\tau)$ and student distribution $\mathrm{softmax}_{\Omega}(c^S_{i\cdot}/\tau_S)$.
(i) Over score assignments $c^S_{i\cdot}$ the term has infimum $0$, attained exactly on the affine family $c^S_{ij}=(\tau_S/\tau)\,c^T_{ij}+a_i$ on $\Omega$ --- a shift family when $\tau_S=\tau$ and a rescaling otherwise.
(ii) Suppose the ambient term at the same row also vanishes, on a set $\Omega_0$ such that $\Omega\cap\Omega_0$ contains columns $j,j'$ with $c^T_{ij}\neq c^T_{ij'}$. Then $\tau_S=\tau$.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Proof.}
(i) If the divergence vanishes, the two softmax distributions coincide on $\Omega$, and taking log-ratios of any two columns $j,j'\in\Omega$ (as in Appendix~\ref{app:proof-gauge}) gives
\begin{equation}
    \frac{c^S_{ij}-c^S_{ij'}}{\tau_S}
    =\frac{c^T_{ij}-c^T_{ij'}}{\tau},
\end{equation}
so $c^S_{ij}-(\tau_S/\tau)\,c^T_{ij}$ is constant on $\Omega$; call it $a_i$. Conversely, any score assignment of that form produces identical softmax distributions and zero divergence, so the infimum $0$ is attained exactly on the stated family.

(ii) The ambient term carries the same temperature $\tau_0$ on both sides, so its vanishing gives, by the shift case of (i), $c^S_{ij}=c^T_{ij}+b_i$ on $\Omega_0$. Combining with (i) on the overlap,
\begin{equation}
    \Big(1-\frac{\tau_S}{\tau}\Big)\,c^T_{ij}=a_i-b_i
    \qquad\text{for every } j\in\Omega\cap\Omega_0,
\end{equation}
and evaluating at two columns with $c^T_{ij}\neq c^T_{ij'}$ forces $\tau_S=\tau$. \qed

Part (ii) is the operative statement: with the ambient scale in the objective, a softmax-of-cosines target must be matched \emph{at its own temperature}, or the two zero sets are disjoint and the objective acquires an artificial floor. The $r=1$ target is the transition row $P^T_i$, a softmax of teacher cosines at the bandwidth $\tau_i$, so $\tau_1(i)=\tau_i$; the walk targets are transition rows as well, so $\tau_w(j)=\tau_j$, which is the tie Proposition~\ref{prop:walkgauge} assumes. The proposition is stated for one row and constrains only the \emph{tie}, saying nothing about the value of $\tau$, so it applies unchanged when every row carries its own bandwidth. The ambient scale uses one $\tau_0$ on both sides, the same-temperature convention of knowledge distillation~\citep{hinton2015distilling}, which by (i) makes its zero set exactly the shift family of Proposition~\ref{prop:gauge}.

\subsection{The rest of the temperature ladder}
\label{app:hyper-ladder}

The tie fixes $\tau_1$; the broader diffusion scales are then set by $\tau_r=\sqrt{r}\,\tau_1$, the $\sqrt{t}$ spread of a diffusion at nominal time $r$. We state this as a rule rather than derive it: the lazy walk takes $r/2$ real steps in expectation, so a strict derivation would carry a different constant, and at $r=1$ the tie fixes the value regardless. What the rule buys is that the ladder has no free entries and inherits the per-row tie automatically --- under entropic bandwidths $\tau_r(i)=\sqrt{r}\,\tau_i$ varies by row exactly as $\tau_1$ does. The scale weights are set by the same principle in the other direction, $\omega_r=1/r$ with $\omega_0=\omega_1$, so a scale's influence falls off with the diffusion time it describes.

\subsection{Truncation tolerance}
\label{app:hyper-truncation}

Sparse diffusion, the per-anchor candidate pool, and the walk support all perform the same operation: keep a subset of a probability row and renormalize. RIPPLE fixes one tolerance $\delta$ and keeps, per row, the smallest column set carrying $1-\delta$ of the mass. Such a truncation perturbs the row by exactly $\delta$ in total variation and by $-\log(1-\delta)$ nats in Kullback--Leibler divergence---a bound in the units of the loss itself---and by Lemma~\ref{lem:truncation} the error is additive across steps, so an $r$-step target is within $r\delta$ of the exact one. Every stored array width is therefore a consequence of $\delta$. Memory guards cap the widest rows; the build reports if either bound binds before the tolerance is met, in which case the guarantee does not hold.

\subsection{Non-backtracking walks}
\label{app:hyper-nonbacktracking}

The walk of Eq.~\eqref{eq:walk} is non-backtracking: the step at $t+1$ is drawn from $P^T_{j_t}$ conditioned on $j_{t+1}\neq j_{t-1}$, except at a node of degree one, where the edge just traversed is the only one available. Since rows are supervised with weight equal to their visit count, an excursion that returns immediately spends the walk budget re-supervising a row it has already seen; forbidding it raises the number of distinct rows a walk of given length reaches, and non-backtracking walks also have faster mixing and cover time on graphs of bounded degree~\citep{alon2007nonbacktracking}. The constraint changes only the occupancy measure $\nu_B$---which rows are chosen---and never a target: a visited node $j$ is still matched against its own transition row $P^T_j$ at $\tau_j$. Proposition~\ref{prop:walkgauge} is likewise unaffected, since it counts \emph{traversed edges} and a repeated edge contributes no new constraint.

\subsection{Dense rather than sampled walk targets}
\label{app:hyper-dense}

A natural alternative scores the sampled transitions themselves, $-\log p^{S}(j_{t+1}\mid j_{t})$ summed along each path. The walk is first-order and each step is scored on the teacher's own $j_t$, so that path likelihood factorizes into one-step terms whose expectation over the walk is exactly Eq.~\eqref{eq:lrow}: the sampled form is an unbiased but higher-variance estimator of the dense one. Since $P^{T}_{j}$ is already materialized during graph construction, we use the dense form and pay no variance. Nothing in the term couples two consecutive steps; it matches one-step transition rows at walk-visited nodes.
