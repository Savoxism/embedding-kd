\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Embedding distillation.}
Knowledge distillation transfers the behavior of a high-capacity teacher to a compact student~\citep{hinton2015distilling}. Language-model compression has used logits, hidden states, attention maps, and intermediate representations~\citep{sanh2019distilbert,jiao2019tinybert,wang2020minilm,xu2024survey}. Embedding-specific methods instead target the geometry used by retrieval and similarity tasks. DistillCSE transfers contrastive sentence-embedding behavior~\citep{gao2021distilcse,xu2023distillcse}; EmbedDistill aligns dense-retrieval representations~\citep{kim2023embeddistill}; and recent systems such as Gecko, Jasper--Stella, LEAF, and Jina Embeddings v5 combine teacher-generated data, representation matching, or task-specific training~\citep{lee2024gecko,zhang2024jasperstella,vujanic2025leaf,akram2026jinaembeddingsv5}. These methods establish strong embedding students, but they do not directly address which corpus relations an embedding objective exposes to the student.

\paragraph{Relational and neighborhood distillation.}
Relational KD replaces coordinate matching with relations among examples. RKD matches distances and angles~\citep{park2019rkd}, while PKT matches conditional affinity distributions in representation space~\citep{passalis2020pkt}. In their scalable forms, these distributions or relation matrices are estimated from stochastic mini-batches. Consequently, the objective can preserve the relations present in a batch without ensuring that an anchor's teacher neighbors were included. Recent neighborhood methods make the selection more explicit. NRKD selects nearest neighbors inside the mini-batch and transfers feature- and logit-level neighborhood relations~\citep{xin2025nrkd}. BINGO constructs teacher-selected bags of related instances for self-supervised distillation~\citep{xu2022bingo}, providing corpus-informed comparisons but no operator or multi-scale target. CoSS instead aligns feature coordinates and feature-space structure without explicitly constructing neighborhood distributions~\citep{singh2024coss}. RIPPLE is closest to this family, but anchors every supervised row to a teacher-only corpus operator and transfers normalized neighborhood distributions at several diffusion times.

\paragraph{Graph and diffusion methods.}
Diffusion Maps use powers of a Markov operator on a neighborhood graph to expose connectivity at different scales~\citep{coifman2006diffusionmaps}; HeatGeo uses heat diffusion to construct a geodesic-preserving embedding~\citep{huguet2023heatgeo}. Graph knowledge distillation typically assumes an observed graph and transfers predictions or representations between graph models~\citep{yang2023graphkd_survey,zhang2022glnn}. RIPPLE uses neither an observed task graph nor a student graph. The graph is induced once from cached teacher embeddings, its diffusion rows serve as supervision for a parametric text encoder, and inference on unseen text requires only the student.

The distinguishing choice is therefore not diffusion alone. RKD, PKT, NRKD, and CoSS derive their comparison support from batch co-occurrence; BINGO uses teacher-selected corpus groups but transfers single-scale instance relations; and graph KD begins from an observed task graph or coupled teacher--student graphs. RIPPLE instead lets a teacher-only corpus operator select both the supervised rows and columns, transfers its normalized neighborhood distributions across scales, and uses overlapping rows to make independently normalized anchors more consistent. The student remains a parametric text encoder and requires neither the operator nor the teacher at inference.
