\subsection{Ablation analysis}
\label{sec:ablations}

We ablate GGP-KD on the Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B $\rightarrow$ MiniLMv2-H384
setting using three seeds.  Unless stated otherwise, all arms share the same
training data, optimization schedule, 89-candidate relational budget, and
2,048-example held-out geometry probe.  We report the downstream average over
the nine evaluation datasets and the teacher-weighted cosine distortion
\begin{equation}
    \hat{E}
    = \frac{1}{N}\sum_i\sum_j p_i^T(j)
      \left(\cos_S(i,j)-\cos_T(i,j)\right)^2,
\end{equation}
where lower values indicate better preservation of teacher-relevant geometry.

\paragraph{Support selection.}
Table~\ref{tab:support_ablation} isolates how relational support is chosen.
Teacher top-$K$ attains the lowest distortion and the best downstream average.
Teacher-proportional sampling eventually exposes more distinct teacher mass,
but each update has a larger restriction error.  This distinction is visible in
Figure~\ref{fig:support_coverage}: deterministic top-$K$ provides strong
immediate coverage and then plateaus, while stochastic policies continue to
explore across epochs.  The large gap to batch-relational KD shows that local
co-occurrence alone does not recover the corpus-level geometry.

\paragraph{Method components.}
Table~\ref{tab:component_ablation} separates graph construction, multi-hop
propagation, the diffused target, ambient sharing, and the row objective.
Multi-hop propagation and the diffused target substantially reduce geometry
distortion, although the small downstream difference for multi-hop is not
directionally aligned with $\hat{E}$. Removing ambient sharing hurts both
metrics, and jointly removing ambient sharing and the row objective produces
the largest downstream component regression.  The row objective improves the
downstream average but leaves $\hat{E}$ nearly unchanged, suggesting that the
probe is a geometry diagnostic rather than a complete surrogate for downstream
quality.

\paragraph{Sensitivity.}
Figure~\ref{fig:sensitivity} replaces a dense hyperparameter table.  Increasing
the diffusion-support quota improves the downstream average while increasing
distortion; this sweep keeps total candidate width fixed by reallocating
hard-negative and random-negative slots.  The downstream average also rises
slightly with the row-loss weight, whereas geometry changes little around the
canonical value.  We retain the graph-derived $K=23$ and
$\lambda_{\mathrm{row}}=1$ as the method defaults rather than selecting separate
values on the test results.

\paragraph{Where geometry errors occur.}
Figure~\ref{fig:geometry_heatmap} visualizes each relation's contribution to
$\hat{E}$ under a shared teacher-graph ordering and color scale. Batch-relational
KD reduces distortion relative to the pretrained student, but its errors remain
widespread. GGP-KD suppresses error across the teacher-relevant corpus structure,
reducing $\hat{E}$ by 83.1\% relative to batch-relational KD and by 93.5\%
relative to the student base.
