\section{RIPPLE: Global Geometry through Local Alignment}
\label{sec:method}

RIPPLE approximates corpus-level teacher geometry through three operations. A teacher graph selects \emph{relevant} local relations, diffusion gives those relations broader \emph{reach}, and a shared candidate pool \emph{amortizes} both anchor-centered and auxiliary-row supervision. All teacher-side quantities are cached before optimization.

\subsection{From Global Geometry to Local Alignment}
\label{sec:relation-selection}

Let $\mathcal D=\{x_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$ be an unlabeled corpus, and let $t_i$ and $s_i$ be the unit-normalized teacher and student embeddings. For each anchor $i$, let $p_i^T$ be a teacher relation distribution over $\mathcal D\setminus\{x_i\}$. With teacher and student cosine similarities $c_{ij}^T$ and $c_{ij}^S$, define the bounded pair distortion
\begin{equation}
\ell_{ij}=\frac{(c_{ij}^S-c_{ij}^T)^2}{4}\in[0,1].
\end{equation}
The quantity
\begin{equation}
\mathcal E
=\frac1N\sum_i\sum_{j\neq i}p_i^T(j)\ell_{ij}
\label{eq:global-distortion}
\end{equation}
formalizes the global teacher-weighted geometry we ultimately seek to preserve; it is not an objective that we compute directly.

Conventional relational KD instead observes the examples that happen to share a mini-batch. The following proposition makes the resulting mismatch precise; Appendix~\ref{app:batch-proof} gives the proof.

\begin{proposition}[Mini-batch exposure is independent of teacher relevance]
\label{prop:batch-exposure}
Let $\mathcal O_i^{(T)}$ be the examples co-occurring with anchor $i$ over $T$ independent random reshuffles into batches of size $B$. For any teacher relation distribution $p_i^T$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E\!\left[1-p_i^T(\mathcal O_i^{(T)})\right]
=\left(1-\frac{B-1}{N-1}\right)^T.
\label{eq:batch-exposure}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

Thus important and irrelevant relations have the same inclusion probability. This suggests replacing arbitrary batch-local geometry with teacher-defined local geometry. The next proposition explains when a collection of such local objectives is sufficient; its proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:local-global-proof}.

\begin{proposition}[Local-to-global geometry bound]
\label{prop:global-geometry}
For any selected relation set $C_i$ with positive teacher mass, define
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_i
=\mathbb E_{j\sim p_i^T(\cdot\mid C_i)}[\ell_{ij}],
\qquad
\delta_i=1-p_i^T(C_i),
\end{equation}
and let $\bar\epsilon=N^{-1}\sum_i\epsilon_i$ and $\bar\delta=N^{-1}\sum_i\delta_i$. Then
\begin{equation}
\mathcal E\leq\bar\epsilon+\bar\delta.
\label{eq:global-geometry-bound}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

Global preservation therefore reduces to two requirements: accurately align the selected local geometry and cover most teacher-relevant mass. Appendix~\ref{app:proportional-bound} bounds the missed mass under teacher-proportional sampling, and Appendix~\ref{app:concentrated-support} contrasts its exposure rate with random batches.

\subsection{Relevance: Teacher-Defined Local Geometry}
\label{sec:graph}

We cache the teacher embeddings and form the mutual $k$-nearest-neighbor set
\begin{equation}
\mathcal M_k^T(i)
=\operatorname{kNN}_T(i)
\cap\{j:i\in\operatorname{kNN}_T(j)\}.
\end{equation}
Retained edges are weighted by teacher cosine similarity and row-normalized:
\begin{equation}
P^T_{ij}
=\frac{\mathbf 1[j\in\mathcal M_k^T(i)]\exp(c^T_{ij}/\tau_i)}
{\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal M_k^T(i)}\exp(c^T_{i\ell}/\tau_i)}.
\label{eq:teacher-transition}
\end{equation}
The row $P_i^T$ defines the immediate local geometry around anchor $i$. Mutuality suppresses one-directional hub edges while preserving relations endorsed from both endpoints. We choose $\tau_i$ by bisection to satisfy $H(P_i^T)=\log\rho$ whenever the row degree permits, where $\rho$ is a shared target perplexity. Lower-degree rows use their maximum feasible entropy, and an isolated mutual-neighbor row falls back to the teacher's nearest neighbor. This entropy calibration gives every non-degenerate row a comparable effective neighborhood size while adapting its bandwidth to local density.

\subsection{Reach: Diffusion over Overlapping Neighborhoods}

One hop preserves immediate local structure; diffusion composes overlapping neighborhoods to expose broader structure. With the lazy operator $\widetilde P^T=\tfrac12(I+P^T)$, RIPPLE defines
\begin{equation}
g^T_{i,1}=P_i^T,
\qquad
g^T_{i,r}
=\operatorname{Norm}_{\mathcal D\setminus\{i\}}
\!\left(e_i^\top(\widetilde P^T)^r\right),
\quad r>1.
\label{eq:diffusion-targets}
\end{equation}
Here $\operatorname{Norm}_A$ restricts a nonnegative vector to $A$ and renormalizes it. The lazy walk prevents periodic oscillation, while the scales $r\in\mathcal R$ describe progressively broader teacher neighborhoods without constructing a student graph. To keep the cached targets sparse, after each multiplication we retain the smallest support carrying at least $1-\eta$ of the row mass and renormalize. Discarding mass $\eta$ changes the row by total variation $\eta$ and reverse KL $-\log(1-\eta)$, making $\eta$ an explicit approximation tolerance rather than an arbitrary width.

\subsection{Sparse Multi-Scale Matching}
\label{sec:candidates}

Full-corpus student scoring would defeat the purpose of sparse supervision. At a fixed budget, deterministic top-$K$ selection retains a high-mass head but assigns every tail relation zero inclusion probability across all subsequent epochs. RIPPLE instead retains high-mass diffusion entries, samples additional entries in proportion to teacher mass, and adds hard and uniform negatives. The deterministic head controls one-step omitted mass, while stochastic tail selection progressively exposes non-head relations within each cached target. Let $\Omega_i$ be the resulting candidate set. Sets are pooled and deduplicated across a batch $B$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal P_B=\bigcup_{i\in B}\Omega_i,
\end{equation}
so every distinct candidate text is encoded once even when several anchors select it.

The resulting conditional losses are sparse objectives, not unbiased Monte Carlo estimators of the full-corpus softmax: both teacher and student distributions are renormalized on the realized support. Their guarantee is instead one of coverage. Every positive-mass relation in the sampled tail has nonzero inclusion probability, and Appendix~\ref{app:proportional-bound} bounds the teacher mass that remains unobserved after a finite number of epochs.

For a diffusion scale $r$, restrict and renormalize the teacher target to $\Omega_i$ as $\bar g^T_{i,r}=\operatorname{Norm}_{\Omega_i}(g^T_{i,r})$. The corresponding student distribution is
\begin{equation}
g^S_{i,r}(j)
=\frac{\exp(c^S_{ij}/\tau_{i,r})}
{\sum_{u\in\Omega_i}\exp(c^S_{iu}/\tau_{i,r})},
\qquad \tau_{i,r}=\sqrt r\,\tau_i.
\label{eq:student-diffusion}
\end{equation}
The temperature ladder evaluates broader targets at a correspondingly coarser resolution and ties the one-hop student distribution to the bandwidth that generated its teacher row.

Restricted diffusion targets deliberately describe relations within $\Omega_i$, but they do not specify how an anchor should compare with candidates selected by other anchors. RIPPLE therefore adds an ambient profile over the shared pool. At a common temperature $\tau_0$, define
\begin{equation}
a_i^T(j)
=\frac{\exp(c^T_{ij}/\tau_0)}
{\sum_{u\in\mathcal P_B\setminus\{i\}}\exp(c^T_{iu}/\tau_0)},
\qquad
a_i^S(j)
=\frac{\exp(c^S_{ij}/\tau_0)}
{\sum_{u\in\mathcal P_B\setminus\{i\}}\exp(c^S_{iu}/\tau_0)}.
\label{eq:ambient-profile}
\end{equation}
The relational objective combines the ambient comparison with the diffusion scales:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{rel}}
=\frac1{|B|}\sum_{i\in B}\left[
\omega_0D_{\mathrm{KL}}(a_i^T\|a_i^S)
+\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}\omega_r
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\bar g^T_{i,r}\|g^S_{i,r})
\right].
\label{eq:relational-loss}
\end{equation}
We use $\omega_r\propto1/r$ and tie $\omega_0=\omega_1$, normalizing all weights jointly. Thus diffusion ranks the selected neighborhood at several resolutions, whereas the ambient term calibrates each anchor against the broader shared pool.

\subsection{Amortization: Exposed-Support Row Reuse}

Equation~\eqref{eq:relational-loss} centers every local objective on a batch anchor. The shared pool contains many additional texts already encoded by the student, so RIPPLE reuses teacher-selected candidates as auxiliary row centers. Let $\mathcal S_i\subseteq\Omega_i$ contain the candidates assigned positive mass by at least one realized diffusion target; hard and uniform negatives are excluded. The auxiliary centers are
\begin{equation}
\mathcal J_B
=\left(\bigcup_{i\in B}\mathcal S_i\right)\setminus B,
\end{equation}
where removing batch anchors avoids duplicating the one-hop term already present in $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{rel}}$. This construction introduces neither a traversal process nor a separate sampling distribution: $\mathcal J_B$ is a deterministic function of the realized candidate pool.

For $j\in\mathcal J_B$, only part of its transition row may be exposed in the current pool. Define that support and its teacher mass by
\begin{equation}
\Omega_j^{\mathrm{row}}
=\mathcal P_B\cap\mathcal M_k^T(j),
\qquad
m_B(j)=\sum_{\ell\in\Omega_j^{\mathrm{row}}}P_{j\ell}^T.
\end{equation}
The teacher row is restricted and renormalized as $\bar P_{j\ell}^{T,B}=P_{j\ell}^T/m_B(j)$ for $\ell\in\Omega_j^{\mathrm{row}}$, while the student row is
\begin{equation}
q_{j\ell}^{S,B}
=\frac{\exp(c_{j\ell}^S/\tau_j)}
{\sum_{u\in\Omega_j^{\mathrm{row}}}\exp(c_{ju}^S/\tau_j)}.
\end{equation}
Writing $\mathcal J_B^+=\{j\in\mathcal J_B:|\Omega_j^{\mathrm{row}}|\geq2\}$, the row objective is the uniform mean
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{row}}
=\frac{1}{|\mathcal J_B^+|}
\sum_{j\in\mathcal J_B^+}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\bar P_j^{T,B}\,\middle\|\,q_j^{S,B}\right).
\label{eq:row-loss}
\end{equation}
The exposed mass $m_B(j)$ records how much of the original transition row is visible but does not reweight the loss; teacher relevance has already influenced which centers enter $\mathcal J_B$. Rows with fewer than two exposed teacher neighbors are omitted because a conditional distribution on a singleton has identically zero KL. This term does not enlarge $\Omega_i$ for the original batch anchors. Its role is instead to amortize additional local objectives over non-anchor centers using embeddings already present in the update.

The complete objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{RIPPLE}}
=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{rel}}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{row}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{row}}.
\label{eq:full-objective}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Cost and deployment.}
Exact teacher-graph construction requires $O(N^2d)$ similarity work before sparsification, although approximate nearest-neighbor search can replace the all-pairs stage. The stored graph uses $O(Nk)$ edges. Candidate pooling avoids additional encoder passes for duplicated columns and auxiliary row centers; $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{row}}$ adds only their pairwise similarity and softmax computation, approximately $O(|\mathcal J_B||\mathcal P_B|d)$ per batch. The cached graph and teacher are used only during distillation, so inference uses the student encoder alone.
