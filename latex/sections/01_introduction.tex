\section{Introduction}

Text embedding models map examples into a shared space in which semantic similarity can be recovered by simple geometric operations. Their usefulness therefore depends not only on individual embeddings, but also on how examples are arranged across a corpus: semantic neighbors should remain close, unrelated regions should remain separated, and local neighborhoods should connect in a meaningful way. Prior work has shown that pairwise similarities, relative geometry, and global topological summaries can all provide effective distillation targets~\citep{tung2019similarity,park2019rkd,passalis2020pkt,kim2023embeddistill,kim2024topkd}. This makes \emph{global geometry alignment} a natural objective for embedding distillation: a compact student should preserve the teacher's corpus-level organization while reducing deployment cost.

For embedding models, relational KD is particularly appealing because it matches teacher and student similarities~\citep{park2019rkd,passalis2020pkt}. In practice, however, these similarities are usually normalized over examples that happen to share a mini-batch. This creates a support mismatch. A \emph{global teacher neighbor} is selected from the full corpus by teacher similarity, whereas a \emph{batch-local comparison} is selected by the data loader. The objective aims to transfer corpus geometry, but observes relations through stochastic batch co-occurrence.

This mismatch is severe even under repeated reshuffling. Proposition~\ref{prop:coverage} shows that an anchor observes only an $O(TB/N)$ fraction of its teacher neighbors after $T$ epochs with batch size $B$. Under our training schedule, the expected coverage is $1.03\%$: approximately $99\%$ of the teacher neighborhood never enters the realized relational objective. Figure~\ref{fig:motivation} illustrates this mini-batch relation gap and measures the accompanying topology distortion.

We introduce \textbf{RIPPLE} to decouple \emph{which relations provide supervision} from \emph{how they are computed}. A cached teacher graph fixes corpus-level support; diffusion turns immediate neighborhoods into multi-scale profiles; and mass-aware sampling evaluates those profiles on compact candidate sets. Overlapping-row supervision then couples relations across anchors. Each update encodes only a small pool of texts, but its contents are selected by teacher geometry rather than batch co-occurrence.

Across nine datasets and three teacher--student pairs, RIPPLE achieves the best mean compact-student score in all three settings. Controlled comparisons separate the effects of corpus anchoring, diffusion, candidate sampling, and row supervision. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We identify and quantify a mini-batch relation gap that leaves most teacher-neighbor scores outside the realized relational objective.
    \item We introduce a corpus-anchored distillation method that combines teacher-graph diffusion, mass-aware candidate sampling, and cross-anchor row supervision.
    \item We connect the resulting objective to diffusion-geometry preservation and evaluate it across nine datasets and three teacher--student pairs.
\end{itemize}
