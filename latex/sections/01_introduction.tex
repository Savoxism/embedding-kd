\section{Introduction}

Text embedding models map examples into a space whose global arrangement supports similarity search, clustering, and transfer across tasks. A teacher therefore contains knowledge not only in individual vectors, but also in the relations it induces among corpus examples. Distilling these relations should preserve the geometry of the teacher embedding space over the corpus, rather than merely reproduce isolated representations~\citep{tung2019similarity,park2019rkd,passalis2020pkt,kim2023embeddistill,kim2024topkd}.

Directly aligning all corpus relations is prohibitively expensive, so relational KD commonly constructs pair matrices or conditional distributions from the current mini-batch~\citep{tung2019similarity,park2019rkd,passalis2020pkt,qian2022fullkernel,xin2025nrkd}. Reusing encoded examples is efficient, but it conflates a computational subset with a geometric neighborhood: random co-occurrence decides which relations are distilled, regardless of their importance under the teacher geometry.

Our key insight is to approximate global geometry by a collection of meaningful local geometries. Rather than treating arbitrary batch subsets as proxies for the corpus, we center a teacher-defined neighborhood at every corpus example. Overlap among these neighborhoods ties the local objectives together, while their composition exposes structure beyond any single neighborhood.

\textbf{RIPPLE} realizes this principle with cached teacher embeddings and a mutual-nearest-neighbor graph. Each graph row defines an immediate local geometry; diffusion expands it into multi-scale targets~\citep{coifman2006diffusionmaps}. Sparse candidate selection makes these targets tractable, candidate pooling reuses student encodings, and supervision on overlapping graph rows encourages consistency across anchors. The graph and teacher are discarded at inference.

The analysis supporting this design is deliberately compact. We first show that random mini-batch exposure is independent of teacher relevance. We then bound global teacher-weighted geometry distortion by the alignment error on selected local relations plus the teacher mass left uncovered.

Across nine datasets and three teacher--student settings, RIPPLE obtains the best mean compact-student result in all three settings. The gains are strongest and most consistent on semantic textual similarity, whereas pair-classification results are less uniform. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We identify random mini-batch geometry as a relevance-agnostic approximation to corpus geometry.
    \item We introduce RIPPLE, which approximates global geometry with teacher-defined, diffusion-expanded local geometries.
    \item We demonstrate improved relational preservation and compact-student performance across diverse embedding tasks and teacher--student settings.
\end{itemize}
