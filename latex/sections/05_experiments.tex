\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate RIPPLE from two perspectives: whether global geometry alignment improves downstream performance, and whether the gains arise from replacing batch-selected relations with teacher-selected corpus supervision.

\paragraph{Models and data.}
We evaluate three teacher$\rightarrow$student pairs: Qwen3-Embedding-4B $\rightarrow$ BERT-base (109M), BGE-M3 $\rightarrow$ MiniLMv2-H768 (66M), and Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B $\rightarrow$ MiniLMv2-H384 (22M). Following TALAS~\citep{dao2026talas}, the unlabeled distillation corpus contains approximately 15K sentences sampled evenly from Emotion, WiC, and STS-B. Teacher embeddings are cached once and the teacher is removed before student training.

\paragraph{Evaluation.}
The nine downstream datasets cover classification (Banking77, Tweet, Emotion), pair classification (MRPC, SciTail, WiC), and semantic textual similarity (SICK, STS12, STS-B). We report macro-F1, average precision, and Spearman correlation, respectively. Emotion, WiC, and STS-B are in-domain; the other six datasets are out-of-domain. ``Avg.'' is the unweighted mean of the nine primary metrics. Checkpoints and pair-classification thresholds are selected on validation data, and test data are evaluated once.

\paragraph{Baselines and implementation.}
We compare with the teacher, the undistilled student, SimCSE-unsup, CDM, DSKD, Jasper--Stella, DistillCSE, EMO, and TALAS under the corresponding TALAS protocol~\citep{dao2026talas}. We use one RIPPLE configuration across downstream tasks and vary only the component named in each ablation. Appendix~\ref{app:hyper} gives the complete graph, sampling, optimization, and evaluation settings.

\subsection{Main Results}

Table~\ref{tab:main_results} reports every dataset for all three teacher--student settings. RIPPLE obtains the best mean compact-student score in each setting: $78.09$, $77.99$, and $74.91$. The corresponding margins over the strongest baseline are $0.05$, $1.35$, and $1.45$ points; the first is small relative to run variation. The most consistent gains occur on semantic textual similarity. RIPPLE gives the best compact-student result on STS-B in all three settings and leads all three STS datasets for both MiniLMv2 students. Pair classification is less uniform, including lower WiC scores, so aggregate gains alone do not establish the proposed mechanism.

\subsection{What Makes RIPPLE Work?}
\label{sec:analysis}

Figure~\ref{fig:core-ablation} reports downstream Avg., full-corpus teacher--student JS divergence, and held-out eight-step diffusion error along a causal progression. Comparing batch-local KD, random corpus neighbors, and RIPPLE one-step isolates teacher-selected support from the number of comparisons. Adding diffusion and then row supervision tests multi-hop and cross-anchor transfer, while a degree-preserving random graph controls for generic graph regularization. All variants share the student, optimizer, schedule, and candidate budget; Appendix~\ref{app:ablation-protocol} specifies the remaining fairness controls.

\subsection{Topology Coverage and Training Efficiency}

Figure~\ref{fig:mechanisms} tests the two mechanism--cost trade-offs without adding separate main-text tables. First, varying only $k$ measures how broader teacher anchoring changes retained teacher mass and alignment in fixed teacher-rank bands. Improvement outside the directly supervised band is the relevant multi-hop signal; increasing Mass@$k$ alone is not. Second, varying only $m$ measures retained target mass, sampled-to-full gradient fidelity, downstream Avg., step time, and memory. Saturation at $m\ll k$ supports the claim that target concentration, rather than corpus size, governs the useful candidate budget. Definitions and complete sweeps are in Appendix~\ref{app:diagnostic-protocols}.
