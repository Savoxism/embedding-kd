\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Models and data.}
We evaluate three teacher$\rightarrow$student pairs: Qwen3-Embedding-4B $\rightarrow$ BERT-base (109M), BGE-M3 $\rightarrow$ MiniLMv2-H768 (66M), and Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B $\rightarrow$ MiniLMv2-H384 (22M). Following TALAS~\citep{dao2026talas}, the unlabeled distillation corpus contains approximately 15K sentences sampled evenly from Emotion, WiC, and STS-B. Teacher embeddings are cached once and the teacher is removed from GPU memory before student training.

\paragraph{Evaluation.}
The nine downstream datasets form three task families: classification with macro-F1 on Banking77, Tweet, and Emotion; pair classification with average precision on MRPC, SciTail, and WiC; and semantic textual similarity with Spearman correlation on SICK, STS12, and STS-B. Emotion, WiC, and STS-B are in-domain; the other six datasets are out-of-domain. We select checkpoints and pair-classification thresholds using validation data and evaluate the test sets once. ``Avg.'' is the unweighted mean of the nine primary metrics.

\paragraph{Baselines and implementation.}
We compare with the teacher, the undistilled student, SimCSE-unsup, CDM, DSKD, Jasper--Stella, DistillCSE, EMO, and TALAS, using the corresponding TALAS protocol~\citep{dao2026talas}. Unless varied by an analysis below, RIPPLE uses $k=200$, fixed graph bandwidth $\tau_g=0.05$, diffusion scales $\{1,2,4\}$ plus the ambient target, $m=64$ candidates, batches of 32 anchors, and five training epochs. Scale weights are proportional to $1/r$, with the ambient and one-step terms equally weighted before normalization. Row supervision uses four non-backtracking walks of length four, begins in the second epoch, and has $\lambda=0.5$. Additional implementation details are in Appendix~\ref{app:hyper}.

\subsection{Main Results}

Table~\ref{tab:main_results} reports all three teacher--student settings. RIPPLE obtains the best mean overall score among the evaluated compact students in each setting: $78.09$ versus $78.04$ for Qwen3-Embedding-4B $\rightarrow$ BERT-base, $77.99$ versus $76.64$ for BGE-M3 $\rightarrow$ MiniLMv2-H768, and $74.91$ versus $73.46$ for Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B $\rightarrow$ MiniLMv2-H384. The first margin is small relative to the reported run variation, whereas the two MiniLMv2 settings improve by $1.35$ and $1.45$ points.

The most consistent gains occur on semantic textual similarity. RIPPLE gives the best compact-student result on STS-B in all three settings, improving over the strongest baseline by $1.99$, $3.95$, and $3.90$ Spearman points. It leads all three STS datasets in both MiniLMv2 settings. Pair classification is less consistent, and WiC decreases in every setting; the mechanism-specific studies below are therefore necessary to separate improvements in neighborhood geometry from task-family trade-offs.

\subsection{Breadth as an Intervention: Where Does Alignment Travel?}
\label{sec:analysis}

The graph degree $k$ is an intervention on how much teacher geometry is exposed to each anchor. Every run remains corpus-anchored and \emph{only $k$ changes}; candidate budget, scales, row weight, optimizer, and schedule are fixed. We first measure the ambient teacher mass retained by the mutual neighborhood,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:teacher-mass-k}
    \operatorname{Mass@}k
    =
    \frac1{|\mathcal A|}
    \sum_{i\in\mathcal A}
    p^T_{i,0}\!\left(\mathcal M_k^T(i)\right),
\end{equation}
on a fixed evaluation-anchor set $\mathcal A$. Mass alone grows almost by construction, so it is not the result of interest. The diagnostic is \emph{where} student alignment improves. We rank the corpus by teacher similarity and report teacher--student Jensen--Shannon divergence on the fixed bands $1{:}50$, $51{:}200$, and $201{:}800$, as well as over the full corpus. A gain confined to ranks already included by $k$ indicates support fitting; a gain in a band before it enters the supervised neighborhood is evidence of local-to-global transfer. The downstream average tests whether either effect matters beyond operator imitation.

This design admits three distinct outcomes. Monotone in-band gains but flat outer bands mean that anchoring helps only by exposing more pairs. Outer-band gains at small $k$ support transfer through the operator. A late reversal at large $k$ would expose semantic shortcuts from overly broad neighborhoods rather than support the claim that more neighbors are always better.

\subsection{Local Control or Memorized Scales?}

Theorem~\ref{thm:global} predicts propagation of one-step error, while direct scale targets should tighten longer-range alignment; Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse} predicts that a shared resolution cannot identify distinct scales. We compare one-step, shared-temperature, and scale-specific supervision with ambient and row terms fixed.

For a fixed set of evaluation anchors, we construct $P^S$ from the trained student on the teacher support using Eq.~\eqref{eq:student-operator}, and report
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:diffusion-error-eval}
    E_r
    =
    \frac1{|\mathcal A|}
    \sum_{i\in\mathcal A}
    \mathrm{TV}\!\left(
        \delta_i^\top(\widetilde P^T)^r,
        \delta_i^\top(\widetilde P^S)^r
    \right),
    \qquad r\in\{1,4,8,16\}.
\end{equation}

Scales $8$ and $16$ are never supervised, distinguishing transfer from memorization. On a common evaluation pool, $E_{\mathrm{sep}}$ measures error in the pairwise separations among the $r\in\{1,2,4\}$ readouts; it is small only when the student preserves how distinguishable the teacher scales are. Appendix~\ref{app:scale-separation} gives the definition.

\subsection{Is Row Supervision Practically Necessary?}

Only $\lambda$ varies in $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{RIPPLE}}=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{diff}}+\lambda\mathcal L_{\mathrm{row}}$, and the sole outcome is downstream Avg. over seeds and settings. A positive-$\lambda$ lift supports utility; a flat curve falsifies it, while a monotone curve points to extra coverage rather than balanced constraints. Accuracy alone cannot identify calibration. Per-setting curves are reserved in Appendix~\ref{app:lambda-grid}.
