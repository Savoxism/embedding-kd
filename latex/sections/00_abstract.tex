\begin{abstract}
Relational knowledge distillation for text embeddings commonly matches teacher and student similarities among examples that happen to share a mini-batch. This is a poor way to transfer local geometry: after $T$ epochs with batch size $B$ on a corpus of size $N$, an anchor is compared with only an $O(TB/N)$ fraction of its teacher neighbors, while relations that never co-occur are absent from the empirical objective. We introduce \textbf{RIPPLE}, a corpus-anchored geometry-distillation method that chooses comparisons from the teacher rather than from the batch. RIPPLE caches teacher embeddings once, constructs a mutual $k$-nearest-neighbor transition operator, and distills its neighborhood distributions into a compact student. One-step operator matching provides local-to-global control of longer diffusion distributions; direct supervision at broader times tightens this guarantee. Diffusion-weighted candidate sampling retains most target mass while scoring only $m\ll k$ examples per anchor. Because normalized row matching determines similarity differences but leaves a row-wise additive ambiguity in score space, RIPPLE also supervises teacher-walk-visited rows whose reciprocal overlaps encourage cross-anchor consistency. The complete method uses only final teacher embeddings and discards the teacher after preprocessing. Across nine datasets and three teacher--student pairs, RIPPLE obtains the best mean overall score among the evaluated compact students in all three settings, with its clearest and most consistent gains on semantic textual similarity.
\end{abstract}
