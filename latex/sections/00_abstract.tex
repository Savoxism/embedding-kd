Large text embedding models provide strong semantic representations, but their inference cost limits their use in retrieval, clustering, and retrieval-augmented generation systems that must encode large or frequently changing corpora. Knowledge distillation is a natural way to compress such models, yet existing embedding distillation objectives mainly transfer pointwise teacher embeddings, mini-batch similarity matrices, token-level representations, or mixtures of local losses. These signals are useful, but they do not explicitly supervise the corpus-level neighborhood geometry on which embedding models are ultimately evaluated. We propose \textbf{HeatGeo}, a corpus-level geometry distillation framework that transfers the teacher's semantic diffusion process to a compact student encoder. HeatGeo first builds a mutual $k$-nearest-neighbor graph over teacher embeddings for the training corpus, then derives multi-scale heat-kernel diffusion distributions through random walks on this graph. The student is trained to match these diffusion distributions over sampled candidate neighborhoods, while a spectral manifold objective anchors global topology using graph Laplacian eigenvectors. HeatGeo requires only cached teacher embeddings, introduces no architectural changes to the student, and is naturally compatible with heterogeneous teacher and student tokenizers. We evaluate HeatGeo on semantic textual similarity, text classification, and pair classification benchmarks, testing whether corpus-level diffusion supervision better preserves retrieval-oriented geometry than pointwise, token-level, and mini-batch relational distillation baselines.
