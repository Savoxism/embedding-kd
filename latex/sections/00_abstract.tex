\begin{abstract}
Text embedding models derive much of their utility from the geometry they induce over a corpus. Distilling such a model should therefore align the student's global geometry with the teacher's. However, existing relational knowledge distillation (KD) typically constructs relations only among examples that share a mini-batch. The resulting supervision is selected by batch co-occurrence rather than teacher geometry, leaving most teacher-defined neighborhoods unobserved. We introduce \textbf{RIPPLE}, a corpus-anchored approach that decouples relation selection from mini-batch optimization: a sparse teacher graph determines which corpus relations to transfer, while each update evaluates only a compact set of texts. RIPPLE diffuses the graph to represent multi-hop structure, samples candidates from the resulting target mass, and supervises overlapping rows to couple independently normalized anchors. It requires neither online teacher inference nor task labels and adds no inference-time cost. Across nine datasets and three teacher--student pairs, RIPPLE achieves the best mean score among the evaluated compact students in all three settings, with the strongest gains on semantic textual similarity.
\end{abstract}
