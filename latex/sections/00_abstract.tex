\begin{abstract}
Compact text embedding models power semantic search, clustering, and retrieval-augmented generation, and distillation from a strong teacher is the standard way to train them. The dominant relational objective matches teacher and student similarities inside a mini-batch, but a mini-batch is a uniform sample of the corpus: an anchor's semantic neighbors almost never appear in it, so the objective constrains only a vanishing fraction of each anchor's local geometry and delegates the rest to the student's own inductive bias. We propose \textbf{RIPPLE}, which replaces batch relations with anchored local supervision. RIPPLE builds a mutual $k$-nearest-neighbor graph over cached teacher embeddings, treats it as a discrete semantic manifold, and trains the student to match a family of graph-diffusion distributions, each at its own resolution, over sampled candidate neighborhoods---using only final teacher embeddings, with no hidden states, online teacher passes, or task labels. Our theory derives each component from the requirement it meets. Zero batch-relational loss constrains only the pairs a batch happened to contain, a fraction that provably excludes nearly all of an anchor's neighbors at any practical schedule. Matching one-step diffusion already controls every longer diffusion time, but with an error that grows linearly in it, which is what the broader scales are for and why each must be matched at its own resolution. Diffusion-weighted sampling approximates full neighborhoods at a cost set by target sharpness rather than neighborhood size. Finally, the diffusion loss leaves exactly one similarity offset free per anchor; supervising the rows a teacher random walk visits---nodes the mini-batch has already encoded---collapses that freedom to one offset per connected component of the teacher graph. Across nine datasets and three teacher--student pairs, RIPPLE attains the best overall average among compact students in two of three settings and second best in the third, with the largest gains on semantic textual similarity.
\end{abstract}
