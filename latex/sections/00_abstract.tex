\begin{abstract}
A text embedding model encodes relational knowledge in the geometry it induces over a corpus. Relational knowledge distillation (KD) should transfer this geometry, yet common objectives construct relations only among examples that happen to share a mini-batch. Their supervision support is therefore determined by random co-occurrence rather than teacher relevance. We show that mini-batch exposure is independent of teacher relevance and bound global teacher-weighted distortion by selected-relation error plus uncovered teacher mass. This view motivates \textbf{GGPKD}, which approximates global geometry through teacher-defined local alignment. A cached mutual-neighbor graph identifies relevant local structure, diffusion extends that structure across scales, and sparse pooled candidates make the resulting objectives tractable. GGPKD further reuses teacher-selected candidates as auxiliary row centers, adding local objectives beyond the batch anchors without additional text encoding. It requires neither task labels nor online teacher inference and uses only the student at inference. Across nine datasets and three teacher--student pairs, GGPKD obtains the best mean compact-student score in all three settings, with its strongest and most consistent gains on semantic textual similarity.
\end{abstract}
