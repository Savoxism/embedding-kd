\section{Three Failures of a Single Readout}
\label{sec:analysis}

We now state what goes wrong when the objective of Section~\ref{sec:setup} is optimised
through one softmax, and what each failure forces. All three results are statements about
the readout \eqref{eq:readout}; none depends on the targets being diffusion distributions.

\subsection{A single readout makes multiple scales vacuous}
\label{sec:collapse}

\begin{proposition}[Collapse]
\label{prop:collapse}
Fix an anchor $i$, targets $\{p_r\}_{r\in\Rset}$ on $C_i$, and weights $\omega_r\geq0$ with
$\sum_r\omega_r=1$. Let $\bar p = \sum_{r}\omega_r p_r$. Then for \emph{any} single
distribution $q$ on $C_i$,
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{r\in\Rset}\omega_r\,\KL\!\left(p_r \,\Vert\, q\right)
    \;=\;
    \underbrace{H(\bar p, q)}_{\text{depends on }\Rset\text{ only via }\bar p}
    \;-\;
    \underbrace{\sum_{r\in\Rset}\omega_r H(p_r)}_{\text{independent of }q},
    \label{eq:collapse}
\end{equation}
where $H(\cdot,\cdot)$ is cross-entropy and $H(\cdot)$ is entropy. Consequently
$\nabla_q$ of the left-hand side depends on the scale family only through $\bar p$: any
two families with the same mixture induce identical optimisation problems.
\end{proposition}

The proof is one line: $\KL(p_r\Vert q) = -H(p_r) + H(p_r,q)$, and $H(\cdot,q)$ is linear in
its first argument. The identity is elementary, its consequence less so: an objective built
to transfer structure at several resolutions transfers, up to a constant, exactly what a
single-resolution objective on the smoothed target $\bar p$ transfers, so choosing
$\mathcal{R}=\{1,2,4\}$ over $\{1\}$ buys target smoothing and nothing else. This holds
verbatim for multi-teacher, multi-view and multi-layer distillation.

\begin{corollary}[Irreducible floor]
\label{cor:floor}
The left-hand side of \eqref{eq:collapse} is minimised at $q=\bar p$, where it attains
\begin{equation}
    \JS_\omega\!\left(\{p_r\}_{r\in\Rset}\right)
    \;=\;
    H(\bar p) - \sum_{r\in\Rset}\omega_r H(p_r) \;\geq\; 0,
    \label{eq:floor}
\end{equation}
the generalised Jensen--Shannon divergence of the family. This quantity is a function of
the teacher targets alone and can be computed before the first gradient step.
\end{corollary}

Corollary~\ref{cor:floor} makes a tied-temperature loss curve uninterpretable: a plateau may
indicate a converged student or an exhausted objective, and the two have opposite remedies.
We therefore compute \eqref{eq:floor} offline and report the excess $\mathcal{L}-\JS_\omega$;
a near-zero $\JS_\omega$ certifies that the scales carry the same target.

\paragraph{What this forces.} If the objective is to depend on $\Rset$ beyond its mixture,
the student side must vary with $r$. Assigning scale $r$ its own readout temperature
$\tau_r$ is the minimal such change: no parameters, no projection, no extra forward cost.

\subsection{A single readout leaves similarity levels free}
\label{sec:levels}

\begin{proposition}[Under- and over-determination]
\label{prop:levels}
Let $v_i\in\R^{C}$ be the student's cosine profile and $q_i^{\tau}$ the readout
\eqref{eq:readout}.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)] For a single temperature $\tau$, $q_i^{\tau}$ is invariant under
    $v_i \mapsto v_i + a\mathbf{1}$ for every $a\in\R$. Any objective depending on $v_i$
    only through $q_i^{\tau}$ therefore determines $v_i$ at best up to a per-anchor
    additive constant.
    \item[(b)] For $|\Rset|\geq 2$ with distinct temperatures, exact simultaneous
    satisfaction $q_i^{\tau_r} = p_{i,r}$ for all $r$ requires
    \begin{equation}
        \tau_r \log p_{i,r} - \tau_{r'} \log p_{i,r'} \in \mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{1}\}
        \qquad \text{for all } r,r' \in \Rset ,
        \label{eq:consistency}
    \end{equation}
    which fails for generic targets. The system is over-determined, and the minimiser
    depends on the level of $v_i$, not only on its shift-equivalence class.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}. Part (a) is why relational distillation transfers
ranking but not calibration: the student may offset all similarities of an anchor without
changing the loss, so a cosine of $0.8$ for one anchor need not mean what $0.8$ means for
another. Part (b) shows that a bank of at least two distinct temperatures removes exactly
this freedom, without any comparison between the student's metric and the teacher's.

\begin{prediction}
\label{pred:levels}
Tying the readout temperatures should degrade metrics defined by a \emph{single global
decision rule} on cosine --- Spearman correlation over all pairs, pair classification at one
global threshold --- substantially more than metrics using only within-anchor ordering or
fitting a classifier on the embeddings, such as linear-probe $F_1$.
\end{prediction}

This split is what makes Proposition~\ref{prop:levels} falsifiable: an average improvement is
consistent with many explanations, a component that moves level-sensitive metrics while
leaving probe-based ones unchanged with few.

\subsection{Sparse targets anti-learn off the graph}
\label{sec:support}

\begin{proposition}[Support pathology]
\label{prop:support}
Let $p$ be a target on $C_i$ with $\supp(p)\subsetneq C_i$, and let
$q=q_i^{\tau}$. For any $j\notin\supp(p)$,
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial}{\partial v_i(j)}\,\KL\!\left(p\,\Vert\,q\right)
    \;=\;
    \frac{q(j) - p(j)}{\tau}
    \;=\;
    \frac{q(j)}{\tau}
    \;>\; 0 .
    \label{eq:zerograd}
\end{equation}
Gradient descent therefore decreases $\cos(s_i,s_j)$ monotonically, at a rate independent
of $\cos(t_i,t_j)$.
\end{proposition}

The proof is immediate: with $\ell = v_i/\tau$,
$\partial\KL(p\Vert q)/\partial \ell_j = q(j)-p(j)$ and $p(j)=0$ off the support. A zero
target is thus not an abstention but an instruction to separate, and for $r\geq1$ the support
lies inside $\mathcal{P}_i$, excluding every $j$ whose teacher similarity to $i$ is high but
which failed the mutual-neighbour test of \eqref{eq:graph} --- a set hubness makes both large
and biased towards genuinely near pairs \citep{radovanovic2010hubs}. A graph-only objective
is thus trained to push apart pairs its own teacher calls similar, damaging exactly the
calibration Proposition~\ref{prop:levels} identifies. The two failures compound.

\paragraph{What this forces.} A target with full support on $C_i$ is required, and the
family \eqref{eq:family} already contains one: $p_{i,0}$ is a row of the
\emph{unsparsified} kernel, so $\supp(p_{i,0})=C_i$ and every candidate receives its true
teacher mass. The zero-step scale is not a regulariser bolted onto a diffusion objective;
it is the member of the family at which no graph has yet been imposed.

\begin{prediction}
\label{pred:support}
Removing $r=0$ should raise the fraction of teacher-near, off-graph candidates whose
student cosine decreases during training towards $1$, and the resulting damage should
again concentrate in level-sensitive metrics.
\end{prediction}

\subsection{Why not a pointwise anchor}
\label{sec:anchor}

The standard response to ``relational losses do not fix the space'' is a pointwise term
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{anc}} = \frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{i}\big(1-\cos(W_a s_i, t_i)\big)$ with a
freely learned $W_a\in\R^{d_T\times d_S}$. It cannot do the job. A relational loss depending
on the student only through $\{\cos(s_i,s_j)\}$ is invariant under
$G_{\mathrm{rel}} = \{s\mapsto cRs: c>0, R\in O(d_S)\}$, whereas $s\mapsto As$,
$W_a\mapsto W_aA^{-1}$ leaves $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{anc}}$ invariant for \emph{every}
$A\in GL(d_S)$; hence $G_{\mathrm{anc}}\supsetneq G_{\mathrm{rel}}$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:gauge}, Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}). Because the anchor's invariance group
strictly contains the relational term's, adding it cannot constrain any degree of freedom
the relational term leaves free --- including the anisotropic rescalings and shears one might
hope it would fix. Its optimum is the best linear predictor of $t_i$ from $s_i$, fitted early
and then nearly constant. Calibration must come from elsewhere, and by
Proposition~\ref{prop:levels}(b) the temperature bank supplies it within the student's own
metric.

\begin{prediction}
\label{pred:anchor}
Re-introducing $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{anc}}$ should leave downstream metrics unchanged, and
the norm of its gradient contribution should decay towards zero within roughly one epoch.
\end{prediction}
